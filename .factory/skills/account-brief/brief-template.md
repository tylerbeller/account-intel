# Brief template

Use this structure exactly. Keep it tight: this is a working document, and an AE should be able to read it once before a call.

---

# <Account> — Factory account brief

**Prepared:** <date> · **Confidence:** <sourced | partially sourced | hypothesis>

## 1. The play in five lines

- **Account:** size, scale, and the one sentence that describes their situation
- **Window:** why this quarter rather than any other
- **Thesis:** the argument you are making, in one sentence
- **Entry:** the first contained ask
- **Ladder:** where it goes if the first ask lands

## 2. Position and pressure

The momentum and the pressure, side by side, each with sources. Close with one line on why this is a software problem rather than a cost problem.

## 3. Catalyst window

A dated timeline of the last 12 months: appointments, reorganizations, new functions, investment commitments. Mark the two or three entries that make the account urgent.

## 4. Where Factory lands

For each of the three operating patterns, name their actual systems:

| Pattern | Their specific estate | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| SDLC / PDLC maintenance | | |
| Modernization / migration | | |
| Patching, vulnerabilities, risk | | |

Mark any pattern the public record does not support as **unsupported**.

## 6. Competitive field

What is public, what is not, and what you searched. Include the cloud-bundling risk and the trade-offs across integrations, security, deployment, governance, and scalability. Name where a competitor is better positioned.

## 7. Reach plan

| Track | Persona | Opening | Asset | Ask |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Then the timing paragraph: their fiscal calendar, budget refresh, earnings dates, freeze windows, and how you use each.

## 8. First call

The opener, three questions each doing a distinct job, and the close (a specific next meeting with named attendees). Add a short "do not" list: what not to bring to call one.
